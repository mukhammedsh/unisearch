How to use UniSearch

Browse UniSearch to find study programs, admission requirements, application deadlines, and costs. You can use the catalog without completing a profile.

1. Add your details in Profile

For personalized results, enter your study level and subject, annual budget, grade average, and exam results such as IELTS, SAT, UNT, or NUET. Fill in the fields for which you have information.

2. Set filters and select UniFit sorting

Filters narrow the list. UniFit orders it using your profile. Card badges show which minimums you meet, which results are missing, and whether the known cost fits your budget.

3. Review a university or compare two options

Open a university page to check application requirements, deadlines, costs, and official sources. To review two universities side by side, select both on the Universities page.

UniFit: personalized sorting

UniFit orders universities using your preferences, interests, budget, and available information about whether you meet admission requirements. The “Minimum requirements match” score covers published minimum requirements only.

Annual budget

When you provide an annual budget and the applicable cost is available, UniFit factors in how far the cost exceeds your budget. Potential aid is shown separately from cost. If information is missing for a criterion, UniFit uses the information it has.

Preference sliders

The preference sliders cover research activity, competition for first-year places, cost versus prestige, and city size. Leave a slider in the center if the criterion does not matter to you. Moving it farther from the center gives it more influence. Set your annual budget separately in Profile.

If data is missing for one of your active sliders, UniFit uses an average estimate for that criterion. If a university has no data for any active slider, its score omits the preference component.

Missing exam or language results

A university stays in the catalog if an exam result or proof of language proficiency is missing. When the site can identify what is missing, it shows what to add.

How to see what affected the order

Profile shows which answers UniFit uses. With UniFit selected, the top of the catalog shows the information used for the current order.

Calculation formula 
without semantic matching: preference_mismatch × 0.60 + requirements_gap × 0.40
with semantic matching: preference_mismatch × 0.35 + requirements_gap × 0.30 + semantic_gap × 0.35
affordability_gap × 0.20 is added when annual cost and profile budget are known; available components are normalized by their total weight

How your interests are used

Use the Interests field to enter subjects or fields of study. UniFit looks for related topics in the information about each university.

Calculation formula 
sim = cosine(queryVec, uniVec)
mlScore = clip((sim - 0.55) / 0.35, 0, 1)

Matching uses the university name, programs, descriptions, and other catalog information. UniFit recognizes some common abbreviations, such as ML for machine learning.

Enter specific topics separated by commas. For example, “robotics, game development, computer science.”

Enter topics you want to study. The field does not support exclusions, so “no biology” can still match biology programs.

Interests affect the catalog order only. The site checks admission requirements separately. When interest matching is unavailable, the catalog reports it and uses the remaining profile information.

Minimum requirements match

The “Minimum requirements match” score shows the percentage of published academic and language minimums you meet among those the site can check. On pages with reviewed data, the score applies to the selected study option, application route, and applicant category.

Calculation formula 
requirements_fit = met evaluable academic and language minimum checks / all evaluable academic and language minimum checks × 100

For example, if the site can check four minimums and you meet three, your score is 75%. Published admission rates and average scores of admitted students are separate figures and do not affect the calculation.

A score of 100% means you meet every minimum the site checked. Review application eligibility, documents, and other conditions separately. The score cannot tell you how likely you are to be admitted or receive aid.

When published requirements or profile details are insufficient, the site leaves the score unavailable and explains what is missing.

University comparison

On the Universities page, select Compare from a card or map marker. Choose two universities. Select a university again to remove it. You can compare two universities with reviewed admissions data or two whose data uses the earlier format.

The table highlights differences that matter, such as different programs, entry years, or fee categories. Each row indicates when data is missing or values cannot be compared directly.

For universities with reviewed data, each column uses the selections made on the university overview. Choose a study option in that column if needed. The table shows deadlines, requirements, costs before aid, funding options, and official sources.

The published admission rate may cover the whole university or an individual program. Check the official source to see which applicants the figure covers. The “Minimum requirements match” score is calculated separately using your profile.

How to check admission requirements

Open Admission on the university page. Check the study option and its application route, which is how the university accepts applications for that option. Requirements may depend on applicant category, entry year, and fee category.

Universities may make exceptions or review the application as a whole. Funding opportunities are listed separately. Published annual costs are shown before potential aid.

Language requirements may call for any one of the accepted forms of proof or for all listed forms. Depending on the university's rules, it may accept an exam result, a CEFR proficiency level, or a first language.

Enter scores on the scale on which they were issued. UniFit may convert your grade average for sorting. Checking a published minimum requires the university's specified scale. For JLPT, N1 is the highest level and N5 is the introductory level.

Academic and language requirements

Academic and language requirements are checked separately. If the university sets a minimum score for each, you need to meet both.

Besides scores, a university may require specific school subjects, documents, an interview, or a portfolio. Check these conditions on the official page for the application route you selected.

If the university publishes a range of admitted students' scores, it describes previous intakes. Look up minimum scores for the current intake in the published requirements.

What catalog badges mean

Badges summarize cost, funding, and admissions information on university cards. A missing badge can mean the condition does not apply or there is insufficient information.

Cards with reviewed data display some badges as icons with explanations. With UniFit selected, the icon legend is available above the catalog.

Cost and admissions information

  • The admission rate is the share of applicants admitted according to the university's published figures.
  • “Aid Available” means the university lists a funding opportunity. Check the eligibility rules and amount separately.
  • “Over Budget” means the known annual cost exceeds the budget in your profile. The university stays in the catalog.
  • “Over Budget • Aid Available” means the cost exceeds your budget and the university lists a funding opportunity. Check eligibility and award amounts separately.
  • Check the cost if it depends on a fee category you have not selected. For example, UK Home and Overseas rates may differ.

Profile-based badges

These badges appear when you sort with UniFit. They depend on the information in your profile.

  • “Exam result needed” means UniSearch needs a result in your profile to check a minimum score.
  • “Your Vibe” indicates a very close match to the preferences you set.
  • “Good Match” indicates that the university closely matches the preferences you set.
  • “Requirements Met” means your profile meets all academic and language minimums the site checked. Review other admission conditions separately.
  • “Below Requirements” means your profile falls short of at least one minimum the site checked. Open the admission requirements to see which one.
