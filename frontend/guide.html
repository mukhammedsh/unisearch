How to use UniSearch and understand scores

UniFit (AI Smart Sort)

What it optimizes

UniFit balances prestige, affordability, and your admission feasibility.

Slider behavior

Budget → Prestige controls preference, but impossible tracks are still penalized.

Requirements logic

Higher scores improve fit; scores below minimum strongly reduce admission chance.

UniChance (0-100 Admission Probability)

UniChance estimates your probability to enter a specific university and track using your current profile.

What it uses

Exams, language proof, track minimums, typical admitted scores, selectivity, and affordability context.

How to interpret

80-100: high chance • 60-79: good chance • 40-59: moderate • 0-39: low.

Best Track logic

The system evaluates each admission track and shows the strongest path as your best UniChance result.

Admission Track Terms

Requirements

Minimum scores needed to apply for the track.

Real Average (Admitted)

Typical scores of accepted students; target this for safer chances.

Language Track Rules

Mode any: one listed proof is enough. Mode all: all listed proofs are required.

Scholarships

Some tracks include merit/need grants that can remove or reduce budget penalty.

Exam Basics (Admissions)

Exams are used to estimate academic readiness and language proficiency. Most admissions systems combine one or more of these exam types.

National Exams

State-level exams used for local university admission (example: Gaokao, UNT).

International Academic Tests

Used by universities abroad (example: SAT, ACT, IB/AP evidence).

Language Certificates

Required when program language is foreign for the applicant (example: IELTS, TOEFL, TestDaF).

National Exam Structure

  • Core subjects: literacy, mathematics, and basic civic/history blocks.
  • Electives: major-specific tracks (technical, medical, humanities).
  • Cutoff score: minimum score needed to apply at all.
  • Passing score: competitive threshold changing by year and seat count.

Task Formats You Will See

  • MCQ: fast check of foundational knowledge.
  • Context tasks: apply knowledge to text/tables/diagrams.
  • Open-ended: show deeper reasoning or writing quality.
  • Digital trend: many exams now use computer/adaptive delivery.
