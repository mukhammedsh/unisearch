How UniSearch works in simple words

This page explains, in plain language, what profile data we use and how to read your results.

UniFit (smart sorting)

UniFit builds a university list for you. It uses your filters, budget, preferred locations, and profile data.

If exam or language data is missing, you still see options, but we mark that extra proof may be needed.

One university can have several admission tracks. We check each track separately, so your best option may be inside a lower-ranked university.

When you update profile data, the order updates too. This is expected: results always reflect your current information.

How your interests are used

In the Interests field, write what matters to you: major, city, budget, scholarships, campus style, and so on.

The system compares this text with university and program descriptions.

More specific interests usually give better recommendations.

Keep it short. Example in English: computer science, scholarships, USA.

Interests help sort options, but they do not replace official university requirements.

Translation is unavailable. Please write interests in English.

UniChance (estimated admission chance 0-100)

UniChance shows an approximate admission chance. It is guidance, not a guarantee.

The estimate is based on your data: scores, language, budget, and selected track requirements.

Easy reading: 80-100 high, 60-79 good, 40-59 medium, below 40 low.

Check Best Track to see where your chance is strongest inside that university.

ROI (return on investment)

ROI compares the estimated early-career graduate salary with the estimated cost of one study year.

In simple terms, it helps you see whether the financial outcome looks strong relative to what you pay for education.

ROI is a planning signal, not a salary promise. Real outcomes depend on your major, country, skills, work rights, and the job market.

UniSearch shows ROI only when verified graduate salary data is available. If a university does not publish reliable salary data, the ROI block is hidden.

Use ROI together with tuition, aid, admission chance, and program fit. A cheaper university is not always better, and a high salary estimate does not remove admission or visa risk.

University comparison

Comparison is inside Universities, in the Comparing tab. The catalog keeps normal navigation, so selecting universities for comparison happens only in that dedicated tab.

Open Comparing, choose universities from cards or the map, then press Compare. Clicking a selected card again removes it from the shortlist.

The results page scans comparable published parameters: ranking, cost, aid, acceptance, tracks, requirements, common exams, language minimums, bachelor programs, study formats, salary, and data confidence.

Key differences explain practical reasons to choose each university. Overview scores are relative only to the selected universities, and the detailed table highlights the strongest value in each row.

Missing official data is shown as unavailable instead of being invented. Use comparison as a decision aid together with official university sources.

1. Select Use cards or map pins in Comparing.
2. Compare Build a results page from the shortlist.
3. Read Check key differences, scores, table highlights, and conclusion.

How to read admission tracks

An admission track is one concrete way to enter the same university.

Requirements are minimum thresholds. For safer planning, also compare yourself with average admitted scores.

Mode any means one language proof is enough. Mode all means every listed proof is required.

Scholarships and aid can noticeably reduce total cost, so always check track details.

Exam Basics (Admissions)

Admissions usually need two parts: academic scores and proof of language proficiency.

Requirements differ by country and university, so check each track directly.

Language requirements are especially important for international programs.

Simple strategy: first cover minimums, then improve scores for stronger options.

Tags

Card tags help you quickly read why a university looks good or risky for your profile.

Standard Tags

  • Acceptance Rate: shown when acceptance data is available.
  • Aid Available: shown when merit-based or need-based aid is present.
  • Over Budget: shown when estimated annual cost is above your profile budget.
  • Over Budget • Aid Available: shown when cost is above budget but aid exists.

AI Tags

Tags that work only when UniFit selected as sort strategy.

  • Conditional / Exam Needed: shown when required exam evidence is incomplete for a track.
  • Your Vibe: shown for very strong preference match (focus, atmosphere, location).
  • Good Match: shown for a good preference match that does not reach Your Vibe level.
  • Likely Grant: shown when grant-oriented chance is strong.
  • Paid Admission: shown when general admission chance is strong in paid route.
  • Requirements Met: shown when minimum requirements are satisfied.
  • Below Requirements: shown when minimum requirements are not satisfied.
