Glossary

UniFit (smart sorting)

UniFit sorts the catalog using profile preferences, fit with published academic and language requirements, and interest relevance. Its ranking score is separate from UniChance, which is only the requirements fit component:

mismatch = (|practice - uni| + |social - uni| + |city - uni| + |budget - uni|) / 4

Change anything in the profile — an exam score, the budget, a slider — and the list is recomputed from scratch, so what you see always reflects your current data. Each ingredient is scaled from 0 to 1.

Focus, Atmosphere, Finance, and Location are preference sliders. The Finance slider expresses a preference between budget and prestige; it is not your budget amount and does not estimate grant or paid admission odds.

Preference badges describe slider alignment. Requirement badges describe whether the current profile meets applicable published minimums. Neither predicts an admission decision or funding award.

Missing exam or language evidence can make a requirements fit score unavailable. The university remains in the catalog, and the missing evidence is shown separately.

How your interests are used

Interests is a free-text query, not a set of tags. The system converts your text and the text of every university into vectors and compares their cosine similarity, rescaled into a 0–1 score:

mlScore = clip((sim + offset - 0.55) / 0.35, 0, 1)

That similarity becomes the semantic third of the UniFit score. Each university is represented by its name, programs, tags, description, and admission labels, and everyday abbreviations are expanded before matching.

The query works best as short terms separated by commas: computer science, robotics, game development. Specificity pays off, while every extra filler word dilutes the signal.

Full sentences add noise, and negations fail completely: "no biology" still matches biology, because the model has no notion of exclusion.

Interests only reorder the list; they never override requirements or requirements fit. If the model itself is unavailable, interests change nothing at all.

UniChance (fit to published requirements, 0–100)

UniChance is a 0–100 fit score for your profile against evaluable published academic and language minimums for the selected requirement profile. It is not an admission probability and does not predict a funding award.

requirements_fit = met evaluable academic and language minimum checks / all evaluable academic and language minimum checks × 100

The score counts applicable published minimum checks that can be evaluated from your profile. Official acceptance rates and admitted-student averages are separate statistics; they do not turn this fit score into admission odds.

The score is a checklist fit index, not a calibrated probability or confidence range. A higher value means more of the evaluable published minimum checks are met; it does not mean that admission is more likely by the same percentage.

If requirements or profile evidence are insufficient to evaluate a meaningful set of checks, UniChance shows no score and explains what is missing. Missing evidence is not treated as zero.

ROI (return on investment)

ROI answers one narrow question: how does the money coming back compare to the money going in. It divides the estimated early-career salary of graduates by the cost of one study year:

roi = salary / yearCost

The cost is the cheapest admission choice total for your study mode, converted into one currency. The salary comes only from verified graduate data: your selected major when published, otherwise the average across published majors.

When a university publishes no usable salary data, the ROI block simply disappears instead of guessing. Missing data is never plotted as zero.

Treat the number as a planning ratio rather than a promise: your program, country, skills, and right to work shape the real outcome far more than any average.

ROI does not describe admission eligibility. Read it alongside UniChance, published acceptance statistics, and the full yearly cost.

University comparison

Comparing lives on the Universities page in its own Comparing tab, while the rest of the catalog keeps working as usual — only this tab collects a shortlist. Pick universities from cards or map pins and press Compare, and click a picked card again to remove it.

The results table only ever compares like with like: the same academic year, the same fee status, the same scope. Each row then ends with an honest verdict — an advantage for one side, a trade-off, parity, incomparable data, or simply missing data — and the page never crowns an overall winner.

Published acceptance rates are institution or course statistics, depending on their source. They are separate from your UniChance requirements fit score, which compares your profile with evaluable published academic and language minimums. Follow each figure back to its cited official source.

How admission choices work

Universities do not admit "in general" — they admit onto routes. A category is one such route; inside it sit requirement profiles, the score variants of that route; and a funding option completes the picture. Category plus profile plus funding is called a choice, and everything on this site is computed per choice.

Published minimums describe route-specific requirements, but exceptions or holistic review may apply. Published admitted-student score ranges are historical context, not cutoffs or guarantees.

Language proof follows the same logic with one extra switch. Mode any means a single proof from the list is enough; mode all means every listed proof is required. A native language counts wherever the route accepts it.

Scores from different countries are first brought to common scales — GPA is normalized to 4.0 before anything is computed. Higher is better for every exam with a single exception, JLPT, where a lower number means stronger Japanese.

What admissions check

Every admission choice runs two independent checks: your academic scores and your language proof. The two never compensate for each other — a brilliant math score will not cover a missing IELTS.

Thresholds are set per university, per category, and per profile, which is why two tracks at the same university can demand completely different scores. Read the selected choice itself.

Check the published minimums for your selected route and profile. Compare admitted-student ranges only as historical context; they are not requirements and do not predict an individual outcome.

Tags

Tags compress the verdict onto the card so that a dozen universities can be scanned without opening each one. Standard tags state published facts, while AI tags interpret your profile.

Standard Tags

  • Acceptance Rate: official selectivity, wherever the university publishes one.
  • Aid Available: the university runs at least one grant track worth opening.
  • Over Budget: the estimated year cost runs above your budget — the gap is explicit.
  • Over Budget • Aid Available means the published cost exceeds your budget and a funding option exists. Its award and amount are not confirmed.

AI Tags

AI tags appear only under UniFit sorting, because only that mode computes the numbers behind them.

  • Conditional / Exam Needed: a required exam still has no evidence in your profile.
  • Your Vibe: near-perfect preference alignment — mismatch of 0.14 or less.
  • Good Match: mismatch of 0.22 or less, a solid fit short of Your Vibe.
  • Requirements Met means all evaluable published academic and language minimums pass on your current data; other admission conditions may still apply.
  • Below Requirements means at least one evaluable published minimum is unmet; see the requirements fit section for details.
